# Writing: generating and editing

Covers writing new pieces (stories, essays, scripts, poems, speeches, product copy, worldbuilding) and editing or revising text the user already has. The two work differently, so each has its own section.

## Generating

**Usually fixed:** purpose, audience, length, facts, required content, the user's own voice if you're writing as them, and format constraints.

**Usually open:** point of view, tense, structure, the opening move, tone and register, the central image or metaphor, setting, pacing, sentence rhythm, and what kind of ending it has.

**Reading the seed:** mix the two methods. Index structure and point of view from the digit sequence, and read the rest associatively:

- Run lengths in the seed suggest sentence length: long runs mean long, rolling sentences; frequent breaks mean short, clipped ones.
- Case rhythm suggests where the emphasis lands.
- The longest letter runs suggest a central image, a place, or a name.
- The digit sum can set a word budget for the opening or the number of sections.

**Structure**
0 linear · 1 start in the middle of the action · 2 reverse chronology · 3 a story within a frame · 4 numbered list · 5 letters or documents · 6 two braided threads · 7 one scene in real time · 8 dialogue or Q&A only · 9 circular (ends where it began)

**Point of view**
0–1 first person · 2 second person · 3–4 close third person · 5 omniscient · 6 collective "we" · 7 an object or place narrating · 8 an unreliable narrator · 9 a document voice (report, manual, transcript)

**Building:** write the draft, then reread it against the brief. For scripts, speeches, ads and podcast reads, a voiceover generated with fal (see `fal.md`) lets the user hear the piece. Offer it, and don't make it unasked. The default voice creeps back in recognizable ways: stock phrases, a neat moral at the end, everything grouped in threes, an explanation of what the reader just felt. Cut anything that could appear in any piece on this topic.

## Editing

When you edit, the text's meaning and facts are fixed, and so is the author's voice unless they asked you to change it. **The seed steers how you revise, not what the text says.** It exists so you don't apply the same generic polish every time.

**Revision lens** (two or three per variant, taken from the digit sequence)
0 cut 30% · 1 lead with the strongest point · 2 sharpen verbs and remove abstractions · 3 vary the rhythm (mix sentence lengths) · 4 make it concrete (examples, numbers, sensory detail) · 5 rework the opening and the close · 6 shift the register (only if the user allowed it) · 7 address one reader directly · 8 strip hedges and qualifiers · 9 reorder for tension and payoff

If the user asked for light edits, apply the lenses lightly. Never add facts, quotes, or claims.

**Deliverable for editing:** one revised version per variant, each labeled with a short description of its approach. Add a brief note on the most important changes, or tracked changes if the user works that way. Recommend one version, or suggest combining the best parts of two.

## Deliverable for generating

The piece itself, plus a short note on the direction it takes. The seed stays out of the text, the title, and any notes the user will publish.
