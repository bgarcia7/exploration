# Research and analysis

Covers research plans, generating hypotheses, literature exploration, user-research question sets, experiment design, angles for exploring data, and competitive analysis.

## What the seed is for here

Research tends to start with the same first searches and the same framing, so it finds what everyone else found. The seed chooses **where to look and which frame to try**. It never chooses what you conclude, because the evidence does that. A seeded angle that turns up nothing is still a finding: report it as one, and never stretch evidence to make the angle pay off.

## Fixed vs open

**Usually fixed:** the question itself, rigor, honest sourcing and citations, research ethics (consent, privacy), and the time budget.

**Usually open:** the framing, which angle to try first, which kinds of sources to use, the method, what to compare against, and which assumption to test.

## Reading the seed

Index from the digit sequence: two or three angles per seed, plus one or two source types per angle.

**Angle**
0 historical (how did we get here?) · 1 incentives and economics · 2 user behavior · 3 technical constraints · 4 regulation and policy · 5 contrarian (the strongest case against the consensus) · 6 analogy from a neighboring field · 7 quantitative, data first · 8 extreme users and edge cases · 9 second-order and future effects

**Source type**
0 primary documents and specs · 1 academic papers · 2 practitioner write-ups and postmortems · 3 forums and communities · 4 datasets · 5 patents · 6 talks, interviews, podcasts · 7 code and repositories · 8 news archives · 9 the products themselves (use them)

## Building

- **For a plan:** ordered angles, the specific queries or methods for each, and for every angle the result that would change your mind.
- **When you run the research:** findings with sources, how confident you are in each, and where the angles agree or conflict. Flag angles that came up empty.
- Close with what the conventional framing would have missed. That's what this exploration was for. If it missed nothing, say so.

## Deliverable

A research plan or a findings report with citations. The seed and the menu picks stay out of it, but name the angles plainly so the user can see how the ground was covered.
