# Naming

Covers names for products, companies, features, projects, characters, and places, plus taglines and slogans.

## Fixed vs open

**Usually fixed:** pronounceability for the target audience; no clash with a competitor's name; what the name means in key languages; length limits; and any availability checks the user cares about (domains, app stores, trademarks). Never claim a name is available without actually checking.

**Usually open:** sound (hard or soft, short or flowing), number of syllables, how the name is built, the root language, the field its metaphor comes from, and tone.

## Reading the seed

This is the domain where the seed's literal letters matter most, because names are made of sound.

- **Sound palette:** the longest letter runs supply consonants and vowels to build from. A run like "kRtZ" points to hard stops, and "aeLou" points to open, liquid sounds. You don't have to use the letters directly; take their texture.
- **Length:** the digital root sets a target syllable count. Clamp it to a sensible range for the kind of name.
- **Construction:** pick from the digit sequence, two or three kinds per seed.
- **Metaphor field:** take a domain whose first letter matches the first letter of a letter run, chosen for its associations (T might give tides, textiles, or thresholds).

**Construction**
0 a real word used in a new context · 1 compound · 2 blend (two words fused) · 3 invented, built from sound · 4 an altered real word · 5 a root from another language · 6 a person's or place's name · 7 an acronym that reads as a word · 8 a phrase or verb as the name · 9 a hybrid with a number or symbol

## Building

1. Generate 20–40 raw candidates across the chosen constructions and the sound palette.
2. Filter them against the fixed criteria. Say them out loud in your head: can someone hear it once and spell it?
3. Shortlist 5–10. For each, give the name, how to say it, how it was built, why it fits, and its risks (unwanted meanings, names that sound too close, how hard it is to search for).
4. Names live in the ear, so when fal is available, offer a short text-to-speech read of the shortlist (see `fal.md`). Use the best voice model available.

## Deliverable

The shortlist, then a short list of runners-up. Be explicit about which checks you ran (a domain lookup, an app store search) and which still need doing.
