# Idea generation

Covers product and feature ideas, business ideas, ways to solve a problem, content and event ideas, game mechanics, gifts, and any "what could we do about X" question.

## What the seed is for here

Brainstorms gather around the first five obvious ideas. The seed assigns thinking lenses you wouldn't pick yourself, which pushes the idea pool into new territory. Volume comes first and selection second: the seed widens the pool, and judgment picks from it.

## Fixed vs open

**Usually fixed:** the goal or the problem, real constraints (money, time, skills, ethics, the platform), and anything the user has already ruled out.

**Usually open:** the lens, the domain to borrow an analogy from, the target user and moment, the scale, and the mechanism.

## Reading the seed

Index lenses from the digit sequence, taking two or three per seed. Choose the analogy domain associatively: take the first letter of each of the longest letter runs and pick a field that starts with that letter and has nothing to do with the problem (K might give kitchens, kindergartens, or knitting; Q might give quarries or quilting). The more distant the field, the more useful the analogy.

**Lens**
0 invert it (do the opposite of the norm) · 1 remove the core thing everyone assumes · 2 change the scale, 10× bigger or 10× smaller · 3 borrow from the analogy domain · 4 combine two existing things · 5 change who does the work (the user, a community, software, nobody) · 6 change when it happens (before, during, after; one-off vs. ongoing) · 7 impose a harsh constraint ($0, one day, no screen) · 8 design for the non-user · 9 make the hidden visible, or the visible hidden

## Building

1. **Diverge:** generate 5–8 raw ideas per lens quickly, without judging them yet.
2. **Converge:** pick the strongest 3–5 across all lenses. "Strongest" means likely to work for the stated goal, not merely the most unusual. Drop the duds honestly.
3. **Develop** each pick: what it is, who it's for, why it might work, the biggest risk, and the cheapest test.

If the most useful idea came from a lens almost by accident, say so. It's an honest signal about which direction has energy.

## Deliverable

The developed shortlist up front, followed by a compact list of the raw ideas, grouped by lens and without naming the seed, so the user can mine it for more.
